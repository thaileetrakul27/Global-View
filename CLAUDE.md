# Global View
A light, clean website with an interactive globe. Clicking a country lists the most popular news stories in that country right now, with a link to each publisher.

## Rules
- Next.js with TypeScript, static export only (output: 'export'). No server code in the website.
- Tailwind CSS. Light theme only. Background #FAFAF7, white cards, grey borders, no heavy shadows.
- The website reads only JSON from public/data. All fetching lives in /pipeline and runs in GitHub Actions.
- A country with a Google News edition gets its news from that edition's top stories feed and its national and world sections, plus a Bing News search in the country's own press language where Bing has a feed for that market (all but China); a Bing search alone never replaces an edition's list. Headlines are grouped by story without a model (by how alike their titles are), and the list is ranked by how recent each story is, how many outlets carry it and Google's own top-stories position, so fresh, widely carried stories come first and old ones sink. Stories Google files under Technology or Entertainment (its section feeds, read for labels only) and not under national or world news are down-ranked. At most three stories per country are marked as trending: fresh (under six hours), widely carried, among Google's ten top headlines and not tech or entertainment. Countries with no edition get "world coverage" instead: a Bing News search in the country's own press language (pipeline/config/languages.json) for what its own outlets publish, followed by a Google News search in English, clearly labelled as a search, drawn a shade paler on the globe, and left out of shared stories and "who reads about whom"; their results are grouped and ranked the same way. Stories read in several countries are shown as "Trending today". Everything refreshes every two hours (.github/workflows/update-news.yml). Show headline, outlet, time and link only. Never store or republish article text.
- The Claude API (Haiku by default) is used for exactly four things: translating headlines into English and noting which countries they are about; grouping headlines that describe the same event across countries; spotting natural disasters in the site's own headlines; and researching each ongoing disaster with web search (affected places, a short summary, key figures, candidate donation pages). Each headline is translated once and cached by link in cache/headlines.json; research is cached per disaster in cache/research.json. Nothing else is sent to a model.
- All Claude API use is capped at $50 a month. pipeline/lib/spend.ts records every call's real cost in cache/spend.json and paces web research per day; once the month's spend nears the cap, model calls stop and the feeds still publish.
- Every model call is optional. Without ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, or with --no-ai, the feeds still publish and the previous shared-story file is kept.
- A failed fetch keeps the previous data for that country; nothing is ever replaced with an empty list.
- The disaster map (public/data/disasters.json, built by pipeline/lib/disasters/) shows significant and severe natural disasters that are ongoing now (minor ones are left out; one spotted only in headlines is rated by the death toll its headlines report, read by the model that spots it, and a widely carried story never raises a feed's own rating): earthquakes, tsunamis, cyclones, storms, floods, landslides, wildfires, volcanoes, droughts, heat and cold waves, dust storms. Sources: GDACS (UN and European Commission) with its affected-area shapes, USGS earthquakes with ShakeMap, NASA EONET, IFRC GO operations, and disasters spotted in the site's own headlines. Affected cities and districts come from the sources or from web research, and are placed with OpenStreetMap Nominatim (one request a second, cached in cache/geocode.json, credited on the site). Each disaster is drawn on the globe as an animated effect over its real area; for hazards on land, parts of a source's area that are almost all sea (offshore island districts and their waters) are left out.
- Donation pages are never chosen by hand and never by a model alone. Direct giving is listed first, as "Directly to people affected": GoFundMe's own verified crisis pages (gofundme.com/c/act), matched to a disaster by fixed rule (whole country or place names, the same hazard and a publish date near the disaster's start), with their verified fundraisers and vetted partners; GlobalGiving projects found by web research, accepted only by name, hazard and date rules; and charities', local groups' or schools' own appeal pages for that very disaster, found by web research (or by the other searches, then held to the same rules), accepted only when they pass URL rules (no crowdfunding, blog, news, social or look-alike official hosts), a live check (a donation word and the disaster's place) and date checks, labelled with the name the page itself gives and a note that Global View cannot vouch for the group. Other GoFundMe fundraisers are never shown. Official pages follow. They come from official registries by fixed rule (the national Red Cross or Red Crescent society and its page on IFRC's donation platform, UN Crisis Relief's per-crisis pages), or from web research, including a search limited to those official sites for pages about the specific event, which are listed first as "For this disaster"; a researched page is accepted only when it is on an allowlisted official domain (the country's own society, the UN family, IFRC/ICRC, or the country's government domains in pipeline/config/donation-domains.json) and passes a live check, and must be about the disaster's countries (no IFRC appeal for another country, no listing or search page, IFRC platform campaigns only when named for the disaster). The site never takes donations itself.
- Mobile layout must work at 375px wide.
- Keep components small and commented. Write a test for every pipeline function.

## Out of scope for now
Bias labelling was removed from the plan. Do not reintroduce outlet ratings, spectrum colours or AI bias labelling without being asked.
