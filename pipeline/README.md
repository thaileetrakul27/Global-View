# Pipeline

Fetches each country's news and writes the JSON the website reads into `public/data`. It runs in GitHub Actions every two hours (`.github/workflows/update-news.yml`). The website never calls an API.

## What "most popular" means

A country with a Google News edition is read from the edition's top stories, its NATION and WORLD sections (whose links redirect once, only ever within `news.google.com/rss/`), and, for every edition but China (Bing sends its China market to a page with no feed), one page of a Bing News search for the country's name in its press language (`pipeline/config/languages.json`). The edition's TECHNOLOGY and ENTERTAINMENT sections are read too, but only to label soft news: their articles are not added to the list, and their failing only means fewer labels. Google's five feeds are requested two seconds apart (a section's one redirect is followed at once), which adds about six minutes to a run over the 68 editions; Bing is fetched alongside. Junk headlines, repeated links and anything older than a week are dropped.

`pipeline/lib/trending.ts` then groups the headlines by story without a model, by how alike their titles are (shared words, or pairs of characters in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai and similar scripts; a higher bar and matching figures for two headlines from the same outlet). Each story is scored by how recent its newest headline is (halving every 6 hours, with a penalty after 36), how many outlets carry it (counting the other outlets Google lists under an item) and its place in Google's top stories. Soft news loses 2.5 points, which puts a phone review published minutes ago below Google's headlines up to about a day old. Outlet counts cannot do this, since Google lists at most five outlets under any item. After its ten headlines the top-stories feed gives four items to each section in turn (world, business, technology, entertainment, sports, science, health; some editions skip a few). A link is soft when it is in the TECHNOLOGY or ENTERTAINMENT section, or in the technology or entertainment block of the top stories (its 19th to 26th items, after the world and business blocks) when that block holds one such link and no NATION or WORLD link. The blocks are used only when the items after the headlines fill whole blocks of four, since a shorter section shifts every block after it. The headlines and the world and business blocks are never soft, even when Google also files one of their stories under TECHNOLOGY. A story is soft when one of its links is soft and none is in NATION or WORLD. In practice the penalty takes soft news out of the list: on 20 editions measured in October 2026 (120-166 stories each) no soft story ranked in the top 50, so none was among the 30 kept or in the shared stories; soft news shows only where an edition has few current stories. The story is shown by its best-placed top-stories headline when that is no more than 3 hours older than its newest, otherwise by the newest. A story is marked as trending when it is not soft, is carried by 4 or more outlets, has a headline from the last 6 hours and is among the ten headlines that open Google's top stories, at most 3 per list (the best-ranked that qualify). World coverage has no top-stories feed and no section labels, so none of its stories is soft or trending. Each run keeps up to 30 stories per country. Grouping runs before translation, so only the kept headlines are translated.

No article text is fetched or stored; the site shows headlines, outlet names, times and links, and tags recent, widely carried headlines as trending (the tag's tooltip gives the outlet count).

## World coverage

Google publishes editions for about 68 countries. Every other country on the globe gets world coverage: a Bing News search for its name in its press language, for what its own outlets publish, and a Google News search in English for international coverage (`"Mongolia" when:3d`; see "Countries without an edition" below). Both are grouped and ranked like an edition's, capped at 20 and labelled "world coverage" on the site. `pipeline/config/world-queries.json` holds hand-written Google queries for names that are ambiguous (Georgia, Jordan, Chad, Niger, Guinea) and a short skip list (Antarctica). Because it is a search and not the country's own edition, world coverage is excluded from shared stories and attention counts.

## What the model does

Four jobs, all optional, all on Claude Haiku 5.5 by default:

1. **Translate.** New headlines go to the model in batches of 40 per country, with the edition named so unnamed places resolve correctly. It returns an English headline and the ISO codes of the countries each headline is about. Results are cached by article link in `cache/headlines.json`, pruned after 48 hours out of every feed, so a headline is never translated twice.
2. **Group shared stories.** One call per run receives every translated headline with its country code and returns groups that describe the same event in at least two countries, each with a short neutral title. Groups are validated: bad indices and single-country groups are dropped.
3. **Spot disasters in the news.** One call per run reads the headlines that name a hazard and returns natural disasters happening now, with the places named and, for each, how many people its headlines report it killed (null when none states a toll; never projections, the injured or missing, animals, homes, ages, dates or another event's toll). An event needs two outlets and a place that geocodes. The model gives no severity: the code keeps the toll only when it is a whole number below a million (`deathsOf`) and rates the event by fixed rule (`newsSeverity`), severe at 100 deaths or more, significant at 5 or more, minor otherwise; how many outlets carry a story never raises it. An event that keeps last run's id keeps the higher of the two tolls, since the headlines that state a toll leave the feeds before the disaster ends. Only significant and severe disasters go on the map, so a minor news event shows only by adding its places and headlines to a feed's disaster it merges with, and a news report raises a feed's rating only by its toll.
4. **Research disasters.** Only significant and severe disasters are researched (minor ones are not on the map). For those that need it most, a call with web search (at most four searches) finds the affected cities and districts, a short summary, key figures with their sources and candidate donation pages; a second call turns the notes into checked fields. Each then gets two short searches, each with its own parse: a donation search, web search limited to official sites (the country's Red Cross or Red Crescent society, the donation platform, IFRC and ICRC, the UN and its agencies; at most three searches) for pages about this disaster itself, with a parse that marks each page as for the event or a general fund; and a direct search on the whole web (at most five searches) for GlobalGiving projects or funds for this disaster and for appeal pages for it on the own sites of local organisations, schools, parishes and other faith communities, community groups and charities (local, national or international) collecting for its families, homes or schools, never their general donate or home pages. The direct search is told to search purposefully: by the event's own name (a storm's name and its local name, or its place and hazard, such as "<place> floods"), its places and its dates, in the affected countries' own languages as well as English, with queries such as "<event> donate", "<event> relief fund", "<event> appeal", "<place> <hazard> donation drive" and "globalgiving <event>". Each direct search logs how many searches it made and how many worked and how many URLs they returned or cited (on the line where it stops early too: no search worked, no pages found, no notes, or notes that could not be parsed), how many pages its parse named and how many were kept, and a line for each page named but not kept, with why (not a URL the search returned or cited, not https, named twice, more than eight). Only links a search returned are kept, and every search's GoFundMe crisis pages (`gofundme.com/c/act/<slug>`) are noted for the donation step. Disasters researched earlier get the search they lack as a backfill, at most six disasters a run. A search that fails is tried again after a day; one that works and finds nothing is done. Results are cached per disaster in `cache/research.json` and refreshed every 24 hours for severe disasters and 48 for significant ones; until a new donation or direct search succeeds, the pages the last one found are kept.

The "who reads about whom" counts come from the translation step's country codes and need no extra call. If a model call fails, the previous data is kept and the step is retried next run.

Set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` to enable the model. `GLOBAL_VIEW_MODEL` overrides the model for jobs 1 to 3 and `GLOBAL_VIEW_RESEARCH_MODEL` for job 4.

## Budget

All model use is capped at $50 a month (`pipeline/config/budget.json`). `pipeline/lib/spend.ts` prices every call from its real token and search counts and records it in `cache/spend.json`, by purpose and by day. Web research gets a daily allowance: what is left of the month after a $3 safety margin and the projected cost of the other jobs, spread over the days left; each run also researches at most 12 disasters (`maxResearchPerRun`), each of which may add one donation search and one direct search (recorded as research), and the backfill is capped at the smaller of six disasters and that number. Once the month's spend reaches $47, the run makes no model calls at all and the feeds still publish. The ledger is saved after every call, and the workflow commits the caches even when an update fails, so a run that is cut off still counts what it spent. Research starts nothing new after 15 minutes, or 40 minutes into the run; the donation and direct searches right after a research are bound only by the 40-minute mark. The run log ends with this run's spend and the month's total. Raise the spending limit in the Anthropic Console to match.

## Run it

```bash
npm run pipeline:update                          # every enabled country in data/countries.json
npm run pipeline:update -- --countries TH        # one country, enabled or not
npm run pipeline:update -- --countries TH,GB --max 20
```

| Flag | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--countries` | all enabled | Comma-separated ISO codes |
| `--max` | `30` | Articles kept per country |
| `--delay` | `2000` | Milliseconds between feed requests |
| `--out` | `public/data` | Where the site's JSON goes |
| `--no-ai` | off | Skip translation and grouping even when a key is set |

Set `NEWS_FEED_ENDPOINT` to point the script at a local stand-in server when testing without the network.

## Failure behaviour

Each feed gets one retry. A country keeps its previous file and its previous index entry when all of its feeds fail, when a local edition gets no answer from its top stories, NATION or WORLD (a Bing search, or the TECHNOLOGY and ENTERTAINMENT sections, alone do not replace its list), or when nothing usable is left while the previous list had stories, so the site shows slightly older stories rather than nothing. The run exits non-zero only if every country fails, in which case nothing is written and the workflow commits nothing.

## Countries without an edition

Google News has editions for 68 countries. The rest get "world coverage": a Bing News RSS search (`bing.com/news/search?format=rss&cc=XX&setlang=..`) for the country's own name in its press language, from `pipeline/config/languages.json`, which returns what its local outlets publish, two pages of about twelve items; and a Google News search in English for international coverage. The results are grouped and ranked the same way as an edition's and capped at 20. Either source may fail; only both failing keeps the previous data. Bing's redirect links are unwrapped to the publisher's own URL. Countries not in the language table are searched in English by name.

The junk filter allows combining marks (Thai and Bengali vowels and tones, Arabic diacritics); an earlier version counted them as symbols and dropped nearly every Thai headline.

## Shared stories across runs

Grouping is redone every run, so each new story is matched against the previous file by article overlap. When at least half of the smaller story's articles match, the old id, title and first-seen time are kept; otherwise the story starts now. This keeps `?story=` links and titles stable while the news moves on.

## Daily archive

Every run writes `public/data/history/<YYYY-MM-DD>.json`: the top five articles of each local-edition country and the ten most-shared stories, as last seen that UTC day. `history/index.json` lists the days. The site's day control steps through them. `npx tsx pipeline/derive.ts` recomputes the attention counts, the "about" files, today's snapshot and the disaster map from the data and caches already on disk, without the model; add `--fetch` to refresh the disaster feeds live as well.

## The disaster map

`pipeline/lib/disasters/` builds `public/data/disasters.json` each run:

1. **Feeds.** GDACS (UN and European Commission) gives earthquakes, tropical cyclones, floods, volcanoes, droughts and wildfires with their real footprints: wind zones of 60, 90 and 120 km/h and the storm track, earthquake shaking intensity, flood extents and burned or dry areas. USGS gives earthquakes of magnitude 5.5 and more (or significant, or with a PAGER alert) with ShakeMap contours for the large ones. NASA EONET gives storm tracks, US wildfires (prescribed burns and fires under 5,000 acres left out) and volcanoes. IFRC GO gives the Red Cross and Red Crescent operations for natural disasters, with their districts, summaries and appeals. Each feed's reports are kept in `cache/disaster-sources.json`; when a feed fails, its previous reports are used.
2. **News.** Disasters spotted in the site's own headlines (model job 3), placed by geocoding the places they name and rated by the deaths their headlines report: significant at 5 or more, severe at 100. When the geocoder has to stop (an outage or its limit), an event keeps the places it had last run.
3. **Merge.** Reports of the same disaster are joined by GLIDE number, storm or volcano name, or distance, country and time rules per kind of hazard; earthquake aftershocks fold into their main shock. A disaster takes the rating of its most severe report, where a news report counts only by the deaths its headlines report, so a story carried widely never lifts a quake GDACS rates green. Ids stay stable across runs. A disaster leaves the map when no feed has seen it active for a while (3 days for a cyclone, 21 for a flood, 90 for a drought; longer for strong quakes), unless an IFRC appeal for it is still open. Every report is merged, but only significant and severe disasters (GDACS orange and red, and their equivalents) go on: minor ones are neither researched nor published, and since their reports stay in the merge and the snapshots, one whose severity rises appears on a later run.
4. **Research** (model job 4), within the budget. Researched places are geocoded with OpenStreetMap Nominatim (one request a second, at most 80 new names a run, cached forever in `cache/geocode.json`, each with the place's own names from Nominatim's `namedetails`, such as เชียงราย for Chiang Rai or مراكش for Marrakesh (its names in the country's press and main languages first, then its name split into one per script, since OpenStreetMap names Moroccan places in three scripts at once), which local groups' pages are matched by; places cached before those were kept are asked for them again, at most ten a run within the same 80), kept only in the disaster's countries and near it, and drawn as areas when the disaster has no footprint of its own. For land hazards (floods, droughts, wildfires, landslides, heat and cold waves), parts of a source's footprint that are almost all sea are dropped: a part stays when Natural Earth's 110m or 50m country shapes find at least 15% land in it, or when it holds the disaster's own position or one of its places. This removes, for example, the Paracel and Spratly waters GDACS includes with Hainan. Areas drawn around named places are never trimmed.
5. **Direct giving**, listed before the official pages, at most six: up to three from GoFundMe, then up to two projects, then up to three charities' and local groups' pages (`pipeline/lib/disasters/direct.ts`). Every one is marked as for this disaster and carries a note saying who checked it. The run log gives each disaster with GlobalGiving or local candidates one line (how many came from the direct search and from the other searches, passed, were refused, and wait for a live check) and each refused page one line with its reason (`Direct page <url>: rejected (<reason>)`, as the official pages' `Donation page <url>: rejected (<reason>)`); lines about single pages, these and the direct search's, are capped at 40 a run, and the run says how many more it left out.
   - **GoFundMe** (no model, so also without a key). GoFundMe's crisis pages (`gofundme.com/c/act/<slug>`) come from its index at `gofundme.com/c/act` and from any such page a disaster's research found; older pages stay online off the index, and stay verified (Morocco's 2023 earthquake page still is). Each is read at most every 12 hours (a week when it matches no disaster), with the pipeline's user agent, at least a second apart and at most eight requests a run, and cached in `cache/gofundme.json`. Pages a disaster wants are read first, each followed at once by its feed; then the others. A failed request keeps the previous data and is not tried again for 12 hours, for a page, the index and a page's feed alike, so a GoFundMe that refuses the pipeline costs nothing for the next 12 hours; a page that answers 200 with no crisis page (no JSON-LD date, or no verified fundraisers where it listed some: a bot wall or a broken page) counts as failed, not as an empty page; a page that answers 404 or 410 is kept as gone and not asked for again for 7 days; a page that redirects to another crisis page is followed and the new page read, and neither is asked for again soon; a redirect to the same page at another address (a trailing slash, a language such as `/en-gb/c/act/<slug>`) is followed at once, at most three times. A page belongs to a disaster by fixed rule, the way IFRC's campaigns do. By name: its slug or heading names one of the disaster's countries and no other, with a hazard of its kind, or is made only of place words from the disaster's title, and names no other storm; hazards are read strictly (an earthquake page is a tsunami's but no landslide's, a flood page no cyclone's, a storm's page a flood's only by the storm's name). So `hurricane-melissa` is Hurricane Melissa's and `venezuela-earthquake-relief` the Venezuela earthquake's, while `wildfire-relief` and `public-officials` are no disaster's. By date: GoFundMe published it (its JSON-LD `datePublished`) at most 14 days before the disaster started and at most 45 days after (120 for floods, heat and cold waves and dust storms, a year for droughts), so `morocco-earthquake-relief` (2023) is no later Moroccan earthquake's and `venezuela-earthquake-relief` (June 2026) no later Venezuelan one's. From it come the page itself ("Verified fundraisers for ..."), the fundraiser its Donate button leads to, and its verified fundraisers, those for schools first and then those for homes and families, leaving out any whose last donation came before the disaster started: their slugs are in the page's own HTML (`data-slugs`), and their names come from the same public feed the page's script reads (`gateway.gofundme.com/web-gateway/v1/feed/campaigns?slugs=...`, twelve at a time). Up to two of the nonprofits it lists as partners follow as projects, with their pages for the event (never GoFundMe's general charity profiles, social links or share links).
   - **GlobalGiving**, from the direct search: only `globalgiving.org/projects/<slug>/` and `/fundraisers/<slug>/` pages whose slug or search title names one of the disaster's countries (and no other) or a place from its title, no other storm, no year older than the disaster's and no hazard of another kind, and names its hazard or a place from its title; a named storm's pages must name the storm (so `help-jamaica-recover-from-hurricane-beryl` is no page for Hurricane Melissa). Something must also date the page to this disaster: the storm's name or a year no older than the disaster's (a span of up to five years such as 2025-26 counts as its last year); a page the search dates earlier is refused. The search engine's own date for a page is no proof, since it can be the day the page last changed and GlobalGiving keeps funds open for years: a fund named only for a country or a place and a hazard, without a year, such as `turkey-earthquake-relief-fund`, `nepal-earthquake-relief-fund` (opened in 2015), `kerala-flood-relief-fund` or `chiang-rai-flood-relief`, is as likely an earlier disaster's, and is not shown. GlobalGiving refuses many scripted clients and dates nothing on its pages, so it is not fetched.
   - **Charities and local groups**, from the direct search, and from the pages the research or the donation search found that the official rules turn away only because their host is on no allowlist (`host not on the allowlist`, which the official step logs as `left to the direct checks`): a charity's own appeal for the disaster, such as Penny Appeal USA's for the July 2026 Nuristan floods in Afghanistan, which the first live run found and threw away, is no official page but may be a direct one. No model is asked again for these, and they are held to exactly the same rules below, live check, caps (at most three live checks per disaster a run, the direct search's pages first) and note as the direct search's local pages; they carry no search title or date. GlobalGiving pages among them are never taken: those searches keep URLs the model wrote without checking them against what a search returned, and a GlobalGiving page gets no live check, so GlobalGiving pages come only from the direct search (and the official step logs them as off the allowlist, not as left to the direct checks). By its address, a page is shown only when it is over https on a plain host, not on a shortener, a social network or portal, a blog, newsletter, site or form builder, file host or archive (Ameba, note, Naver and Tistory blogs, Substack, Mailchimp pages, Webflow, Square, Strikingly, Notion, Jotform, Google Forms, archive.org and the like), a crowdfunding or giving platform (GoFundMe, JustGiving, READYFOR, CAMPFIRE, Waterdrop, Qingsongchou, Tumblbug, BackaBuddy, M-Changa, Virgin Money Giving, Everydayhero, Leetchi, LaunchGood, betterplace, SimplyGiving, Give.asia, Taejai, Kitabisa, Apoia.se, PPL, Yahoo! JAPAN's donations, Cotizi, iSupportJamaica, Fonzip, Juntos, BitGiving, Sharing Happiness, Amal Sholeh, WeCare, CAF Donate, HipGive, nonprofit CRMs' donation forms such as Neon and EveryAction, and their peers, whatever the path), a payment, checkout, money-transfer or crypto service (Stripe and SumUp payment links, Mercado Pago, PayPal, Asaas, InfinitePay, Cashfree, WiPay, Khalti, eSewa, Western Union, Coinbase, Shopify and their like), a marketplace, a news site (listed news hosts, the site's own news hosts, and hosts with "news" as a part of a label, such as `jamaica-news.org`, but not `newstorycharity.org`, `goodnewsschool.edu.jm` or `good-news-church.org.jm`), or an official site (those stay with the official links); not on a host that carries an official body's or a platform's name (a look-alike such as `jamaicaredcrossrelief.org`, `hilalahmar-maroc.org`, `sekijuji-shien.jp` or `uncrisisrelief.org`: the movement's names as its societies write them in Latin letters, the UN and its agencies (UNRWA, IOM, UN Women and the like), giving platforms, civil protection and government words; "gov" only as a part or the end of a label, so `govindfoundation.org` passes, and "un" only alone, before an aid or hazard word or a country's name, or joined to an aid word at a label's start, so `un-jamaica.org`, `un-hurricane-relief.org` and `uncrisisrelief-jamaica.org` are refused and `un-techo-para-mi-pais.org` and `unidos-por-haiti.org` pass); not a home, listing or search page, a person's page (`/u/<name>`, `/@name`, after a language or version segment) or a news or blog path (`/news/`, `/blog/`, `/2025/10/`). A group's own site may put its appeal at `/campaigns/<slug>` or `/projects/<slug>` (WordPress donation plugins do), so those paths count against a page only on a platform's host. Nor may its path name an older year (a number alone, such as Drupal's `/node/2019`, is no year), another country (in any language, as below) or another storm; the search may not date it well before the disaster, nor name it for an official body. Then a live check must pass: 200 HTML within three redirects on the same site; no news article (a JSON-LD `NewsArticle` or `NewsMediaOrganization`; WordPress gives a group's own posts `Article` or `BlogPosting` nodes, `article:published_time` and `og:type` article, so none of those counts); no official body's or platform's name in its own site name, title or heading (the movement's names in the languages of its societies, which cover every society's name IFRC GO gives in English, French, Spanish and Arabic, with Pashto, Tajik, Kyrgyz and Uzbek ones, the registry's society names and their short names from their hosts in capitals, such as PMI and ARCS; the UN, "UN" itself in capitals ("UN Crisis Relief", never French or Spanish "un"), its agencies in the disasters' languages (ユニセフ, يونيسف) and the giving platforms; governments, regional and royal governments (Generalitat, Gobernación, Royaume du Maroc, المملكة المغربية), heads of government's relief funds (CMDRF, "Prime Minister's National Relief Fund"), ministries by their portfolio and civil protection, so "Government Girls High School", a school's "Supreme Student Government" and a church's "Ministry of Hope" pass), and no word in them that mixes Latin with Cyrillic or Greek letters (a "Jamaica Rеd Cross" with a Cyrillic е); no request for crypto (a wallet address anywhere, or crypto by name or ticker such as BTC or USDC on a page that offers no card, PayPal, one-off or monthly gift, so World Central Kitchen's "Donate Crypto" among its usual ways to give is no such request), gift card codes (iTunes, Steam or Google Play cards, or codes to send; a community group's supermarket cards for families pass), Zelle, Cash App or Venmo, money-transfer services or a transfer to a person's own account ("transfer to my account", "deposita en mi cuenta", an account "a nombre de" a person rather than a group), anywhere on the page; no text a crowdfunding platform puts on a person's campaign, whatever the host: an invitation to start one's own ("Start a fundraiser", "Lancer ma cagnotte", "Crie sua vaquinha", "Buat galang dana", "Kampanya oluştur") or a person credited as its creator ("Campagne créée par", "Project by Andre W."); no older year (a founding or registration year such as "since 1952", "Reg. 1995", "sejak 1990", "tangu 1990", "منذ 1990" or "1990年設立" is none; Thai Buddhist years such as 2567, Japanese eras such as 令和6年 and Nepal's Bikram Sambat years such as २०७२ are read as the Gregorian years they are), other country (in English by whole names, and in the page's own language: every country's name from the runtime's CLDR data in its countries' press and main languages and in Spanish, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Indonesian, Turkish, Russian, Chinese, Japanese and Thai, so "séisme en Algérie" and "زلزال تركيا" are no Moroccan earthquake's; a name inside one of the disaster's own places, provinces or title places, such as Panama City or the Jersey Shore, and Georgia and Jersey for a disaster in the United States, are no other country's; names that are common words or saints' names, such as Maurice, Granada or Saint-Martin, are not read), other storm (for a storm, or a disaster whose title names its storm; "Storm Boris flood relief" is a page for floods in Poland; a storm's other names, PAGASA's in `pipeline/config/storm-names.json` and those an IFRC appeal gives in brackets, such as Tino for Typhoon Kalmaegi, are its own) or other kind of disaster in its title without its site's name or its heading; no date of its own (JSON-LD `datePublished`, else its first `<time>`) more than two weeks before the disaster started, unless its title, heading or address names the storm or a year no older than the disaster's, or the page was changed since (JSON-LD `dateModified`) and its title or heading names both the disaster's hazard and a place from its title, as a group's standing donate page rewritten for the disaster does; for a page with no date of its own, no older year in a sentence of its first paragraphs that names the disaster's hazard ("El 16 de abril de 2016 un terremoto..."), unless one there is recent; a donation word; a title without its site's name (by `og:site_name` or by its host's name, since a group's own name may hold a word of the title, as "Chiang Mai Children's Home" does for floods in Chiang Rai), heading or address about the disaster, naming its hazard (in its own language and script too: 地震, زلزال, น้ำท่วม, bagyo) or a place from its title as a whole phrase ("Rio Grande do Sul", never "Rio" alone), and for a named storm the storm somewhere on the page, so a charity's general "Give" page that only names the country is refused; and the name of one of the disaster's countries (in English, its press language or its own main language: ไทย, المغرب), places (with their own names from the geocoder, and their short forms: เชียงราย for จังหวัดเชียงราย) or title places. The page is shown by its own site name (`og:site_name`, else its host) and title, never by the model's words. Verdicts are cached with the official ones, per disaster and per version of these rules, and today's rules apply to cached ones too. The note says it is a charity's or local group's own page for this disaster, and that Global View checked the page is live and names the place, but cannot vouch for the group.
6. **Official donation pages.** By fixed rules only, at most eight, in this order: up to three pages for this disaster itself found by the donation search (never a homepage or a registry page), the IFRC appeal, the country's Red Cross or Red Crescent society on IFRC's donation platform when it is really there (the landing page must name the society), UN Crisis Relief's page for that country's crisis, up to three other researched pages, the society's own website when it passes a live check, and UN Crisis Relief's and IFRC's general funds so every disaster has at least two. The site lists the disaster's own pages first, as "For this disaster". Pages suggested by research are shown only when they are on an allowlisted official domain (the country's own society, the UN family, IFRC and ICRC, or the country's government domains in `pipeline/config/donation-domains.json`; a society site that is one folder of a bigger host, like the Cuban Red Cross on Cuba's health network, allows only that folder), are about the disaster's countries (never an IFRC appeal for another country, such as a Syria appeal for an East African drought, never a listing or search page, IFRC's appeal and emergency lists in any form included, and an IFRC campaign on the donation platform only when its name is one of the disaster's countries or a place from its title and no other country, as `/ifrc/venezuela-earthquake/` for the Venezuela earthquake; countries are matched by whole names, with IFRC's spellings such as Türkiye, so `/ifrc/guinea-floods/` is not Guinea-Bissau's; these rules are checked on every run, so they also drop pages a cached live check passed) and pass a live check: every redirect stays on the allowlist, the page answers 200 with HTML and it contains a donation word in its language. A society homepage that blocks scripts with 401 or 403 without leaving its own domain is still linked, since a hijacked domain would redirect away or fail the word check.

A run never replaces the map with an empty one because of an outage: when no feed answered and no snapshot holds a report, the previous map's significant disasters that are still ongoing stay. When the reports hold no significant disaster, the map is written empty, since nothing significant is happening. The file is about 700 KB for 200 disasters; each disaster's areas are simplified to stay under about 120 KB.
